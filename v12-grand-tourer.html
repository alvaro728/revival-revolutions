The V12 Grand Tourer

Proposed · Naturally aspirated V12 · Manual

Concept render Nothing shown here has been built yet.

BMW built a single M8 prototype and kept it out of sight for nearly twenty years. This is the 8 Series we wish they had sold.

Summary

The short version

The racer
The M8 prototype BMW never sold, and the related S70/2 V12 that won Le Mans in the McLaren F1 GTR in 1995.
What we change
A louvred bonnet, vents in the front wings, flared arches, deep-dish wheels and a reworked rear.
What it is for
Long trips. A naturally aspirated V12 and a manual gearbox in a big, comfortable coupé.
Settled
Nothing yet. The donor car, engine and gearbox are all still ideas.
What exists
Renders, design drawings, and cabin and colour studies. No blueprint or engineering yet.

Background

The one that got away

These photographs show the original cars and were taken by other people. Each one is credited underneath.

At the start of the 1990s, BMW M built a single E31 M8 prototype around a six-litre V12 from the S70 family. The car never went into production and stayed out of sight until it was revealed in 2010.

The engine family went further than the car did. A relative of that V12, the S70/2, powered the McLaren F1 and then the F1 GTR that won Le Mans in 1995.

The 850 CSi ended up as the fastest 8 Series you could buy. It is rare and quick, but it is no M8. This study is our guess at what one might have been.

The car

A V12 and a manual gearbox

We would start from an 850i or 850Ci, which are easier to find than a CSi. The aim is a car that drives like a 2000s M car and still sounds like a V12.

Donor
850i / 850Ci
Engine
Naturally aspirated V12
Gearbox
Manual
Design cues
McLaren F1 curves, M8 prototype details, 1980s–90s proportions

This is what we would like to build. There are no power, weight or performance figures yet because the engineering has not been done.
